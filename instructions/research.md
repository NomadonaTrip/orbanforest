## 4. Pain research - two rounds (grounding evidence for all copy)

### Round A - general web search (search-engine intent data)

- Owners search their symptom, not "AI automation": missed calls, slow lead response, losing customers to faster competitors.
- Key stats: 78% of homeowners hire the first contractor who responds; 5-min lead response is 21x more likely to qualify vs 30 min; 25%+ of inbound calls to home-service businesses go unanswered and 85% of those callers never retry; SMBs miss 40-62% of calls, costing $50K-100K+/yr; 80% of missed-call customers don't leave a voicemail, they call the next business.
- Decision: this pain (missed calls / slow response) is real but is the common answer already owned by every competitor (especially the "AI receptionist" pitch). De-prioritized as the headline driver; used only as supporting/secondary evidence, not the lead emotional hook.

### Round B - forum/human-voice research (Reddit threads, user-supplied, read directly)

Sources (real, user-provided, pasted into chat and analyzed):

- reddit.com/r/smallbusiness - "hidden cost of manual data entry" thread
- reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/1r85qcp - "is everyone else still spending hours on manual data entry"
- reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/1v9mddc - "what's one business task you finally automated" (highest signal, most corroborated)
- (r/travelagents and r/Accounting threads were referenced by the user but could not be fetched - Reddit blocked automated fetch; only pasted-text threads were actually analyzed)

Key verbatim findings:

- Manual data entry cost is not just time: 1-4% error rate (about 40 wrong records per 1000, each taking 3-5x longer to fix than to enter); turnover (lost 2 staff in 6 months, $8k+ recruiting cost); decisions delayed a week behind reality; audit/compliance risk from inconsistent formats.
- Highest-corroboration finding (multiple independent owners agreeing in-thread): the real burden isn't big tasks, it's dozens of tiny follow-ups tracked by memory. Verbatim: "it was not the big tasks that wore me down, it was keeping track of dozens of tiny follow ups... freed up way more mental bandwidth than I expected." Repeated motif: "getting it out of my head."
- Invoice chasing: owners avoid it because "it felt rude," let invoices sit 45-60 days; fixed-schedule reminders (day 7/14 or 15/30/45) fixed it without confrontation, cash arrived faster.
- Other named automatable pains: client onboarding repetition, reconciling payments to orders, updating inventory across two systems, payroll tax filing, expense tagging for tax time.
- Structural insight: DIY tools (Zapier/Make) fail specifically on "non-API" tasks - unstructured documents, messy legacy systems. This is precisely the gap Orban Forest's build-not-wire-up capability fills, and precisely what tomos already proves (documents to structured data).
- Conduct note: r/smallbusiness AutoModerator actively warns against, and the community openly shames, businesses mining the sub for pain points to pitch products. Research insights are for use in Orban Forest's own copy - never for posting/engaging in these communities as a pitch vector.

Reframe this produced: the dominant felt benefit is not "save time," it's relief from mental load / cognitive clutter - a more differentiated, less-competed emotional territory than the missed-call/lead-response space.

## 5. Headline development - full iteration log

### Direction 1: "Revenue leak" (grounded in Round A data)

"You're losing customers you already earned - the missed call, the unread message, the quote you never followed up on."

Pro: urgent, money-framed. Con: closest to the common/competitor answer; risks pulling back toward "AI receptionist" positioning.

### Direction 2: "Mental load" (grounded in Round B data) - three variants generated

- A "Relief": "You shouldn't have to hold your whole business together in your head." Purest mental-load hook, but soft/under-motivating alone.
- B "Stakes/blend": "Everything you track by memory is one busy week away from slipping." Later self-corrected and retracted: on review, judged to be a manufactured/hedged phrase not actually grounded in verbatim research; abandoned in favor of C.
- C "Recognition": "It's not the big work that's burning you out. It's the fifty small things you can't afford to forget." Nearly verbatim from the highest-corroboration thread. Selected over B after explicit self-critique reversal.

Refined C, with cost-of-inaction line added from Round B data (errors/turnover/delayed decisions), used as a subhead extension:

"It's not the big work that's burning you out. It's the fifty small things you can't afford to forget. / Chasing invoices. Re-keying data between systems that don't talk. The same follow-ups, from memory, every day. Left alone, that's not just hours - it's mistakes, missed cash, and good people quitting over the busywork. We find what's quietly costing you, and build exactly what takes it off your plate."

### Direction 3: "AAA / Marketika-standard" iterative craft pass (most recent, current best candidate)

Method: multiple candidates generated, then self-administered harsh critique, then compress/fuse survivors, repeated for three rounds.

Round 1 candidates: rejected. All judged to describe the pain rather than inhabit it; several relied on manufactured stats or cliched metaphors ("filing cabinet").

Round 2 candidates: introduced the "hundred small promises" reframe - treating follow-ups/invoices/reminders as acts of integrity/promises to customers, not chores. Judged the strongest new idea of the thread.

Round 3 - FINAL SELECTED HEADLINE:

"Your business runs on a hundred small promises. You're the only one keeping them."

Companion subheadline (current best):

"The follow-up you meant to send. The invoice you keep meaning to chase. The thing you'll remember later - you hope. We listen until we find the promise that's costing you the most, then build the one thing that keeps it. Not a product. Not a platform. The exact system your business needs, engineered the same way we engineer our own - like tomos, live right now."

Why this won (reasoning to preserve):

- Reframes admin/busywork as kept or broken promises to customers - more original and more respectful than "tasks" or "busywork" language used by every competitor.
- Every clause is traceable to real Round B evidence (follow-ups from memory, invoices avoided "because it felt rude," data re-keyed by hand) - no invented urgency or fabricated stats.
- Sets up the diagnostic positioning (section 1) as the natural next sentence rather than a jump.
- Blind side-by-side against a constructed Marketika-style comparator ("You didn't start a business to become its to-do list.") was judged stronger because "to-do list" is generic SaaS language, while "hundred small promises" is a genuine reframe with strategic follow-through.

Explicitly flagged as unresolved / for further work:

- "Utterly perfect" is an asymptotic target, not a claimed achievement - recommended real-world test: read aloud to an actual target-persona owner (contractor, clinic owner) before locking.
- User requested two more headline variants in genuinely different emotional registers (quieter/wry vs earnest-and-grand) - not yet produced at time of export.
- Audience fork unresolved: revenue-leak framing may outperform mental-load framing specifically with trades/home-service prospects (Tier 1 in Tayo's prospecting plan); mental-load framing likely stronger with appointment-based/professional-services owners (Tier 2). No decision made on whether to segment headline by audience/vertical.
